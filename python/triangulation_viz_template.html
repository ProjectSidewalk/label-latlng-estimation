Bearings intersect: the depth-free survey of Project Sidewalk's distance chain

22,647 street objects, each detected from three or more panoramas. Rays from known camera positions meet at the object — using no depth data, no camera height, no vertical model, no panorama resolution. Every conclusion below falls out of that geometry.

SHEET 01

The site survey — one height makes the views agree

A real curb-ramp site from the paterson run. Each panorama's detection is a bearing ray; the rays meet at the star. The auto-labeler instead placed each detection by assuming a 2.6 m camera — drag the height slider and watch the placements slide along their rays: they only converge at one height, and it is not 2.6. Violet squares are Google's depth read at the very same detection pixels — consistently short of the intersection. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, click a camera to see its actual pixels below — juxtaposed with Google's depth model rendered over the identical window.

SHEET 03

Depth vs bearings: a ratio, not an offset

At 2,639 identical detection pixels, the triangulated range runs 13.8% past Google's depth. The two candidate causes predict different shapes: a detector centroid on a fixed-size ramp is an offset — capped near the object's 1–2 m extent, ratio falling toward 1 with range — while a depth model restating its own ground plane is a scale — flat ratio, gap growing in proportion. The data hugs one prediction and crosses the other.
